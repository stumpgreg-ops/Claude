The Lotus Eaters (from Book 9)

Odysseus is telling King Alcinous the story of his journey home from Troy. After the raid on the Cicones, where 72 of his men died, his ships set sail again for Ithaca.

“I might have made it safely home, that time, but as I came round Malea1 the current took me out to sea, and from the north a fresh gale drove me on, past Cythera.2 Nine days I drifted on the teeming sea before dangerous high winds. Upon the tenth we came to the coastline of the Lotus Eaters, who live upon that flower. We landed there to take on water.

Teacher notes
How the themes develop · discussion key · model response

How the themes develop

Beginning (lines 1–12). The goal is stated: “safely home.” The setting supplies the first challenge, a storm that blows the ships off course for nine days, then brings them to a calm island. Odysseus is patient, caring, and careful: he waters and feeds the crew and scouts before risking everyone. Themes 4, 3

Middle (lines 13–19). The danger looks harmless: the Lotus Eaters show “no will to do us harm.” The “sweet,” “honeyed” plant tempts the scouts, who never return and become “forgetful of their homeland.” Themes 1, 2, 3

End (lines 19–27). Odysseus drives the wailing men to the ships, ties them down, orders everyone aboard, and states the lesson: taste the Lotus and “you lose your hope of home.” His hard choice gets them back on course. Themes 1, 4

Discussion key (slide notes)

Slide 20, I Do (lines 1–4). A storm throws Odysseus off his route home: a major event, because it takes him off course.

Slide 21, We Do (lines 4–6). Nine days adrift: accept either side with a reason. Smart not to fight a sea he can’t beat, or he should have steered for home.

Slide 21, We Do (lines 6–10). Reaching the island is a major event; stopping for water and food lets the crew recover.

Slide 21, We Do (lines 10–17). The scouts eat the Lotus and never report back. Some will say this shows the men didn’t respect him.

Slide 22, partners (lines 18–27, 7 min). He ties the crying men under the benches, forbids the Lotus, and gets the ships moving; still, some men no longer wanted to go home.

Slide 23, exit discussion. The storm keeps him from home and brings him to the island; the island’s Lotus makes men stop wanting to leave, which forces Odysseus to act.

Model response

In “The Lotus Eaters,” Homer develops the theme that giving in to temptation can cost you what matters most. The setting drives the plot: a storm pushes the ships off course to an island where the people offer the “sweet” Lotus. Because the Lotus Eaters show “no will to do us harm,” the danger is easy to miss. The scouts who eat it become “forgetful of their homeland,” showing how pleasure can erase a goal. Odysseus’s response completes the theme: he drags the men “all three wailing” to the ships and warns that anyone who tastes the Lotus will “lose your hope of home.”

Watch for

Topic, not theme: push “temptation” to a full statement. “The Lotus Eaters are villains”: they are not hostile; the danger is inside the men. Who is telling this? Odysseus narrates to King Alcinous, so he may make himself look like a better leader than he was.
