Theme statements to aim for

Accept any complete statement that the text supports. Push students past a one-word topic (“temptation”) to what the text says about the topic.

Primary: Giving in to temptation can make people forget their goals and the people who depend on them.
Also strong: Staying on course through life’s challenges takes discipline, and sometimes a leader has to make hard, unpopular choices for the good of the group.
Tie to the EQ: Challenges do not always look dangerous; the easiest path can be the one that costs us the most.

Stated or implied? Mostly implied (the men become “forgetful of their homeland”), but Odysseus comes close to stating it in lines 23–24: “no one taste the Lotus, or you lose your hope of home.”

Model response (characters + setting + plot → theme)

In “The Lotus Eaters,” Homer develops the theme that giving in to temptation can make people forget their goals. The setting drives the plot: a storm pushes Odysseus’s ships off course to an island where the people offer the “sweet” Lotus. Because the Lotus Eaters show “no will to do us harm”, the danger is easy to miss. When the scouts eat the plant, they become “forgetful of their homeland”, which shows how easy pleasure can erase a person’s purpose. Odysseus’s response develops the theme further: he drives the men “all three wailing” to the ships and warns that anyone who tastes the Lotus will “lose your hope of home”. His decisive leadership shows that staying true to a goal can require discipline and hard choices.

Exit ticket (3-2-1) key

3 major events: a storm drives the ships off course for nine days; the scouts eat the Lotus and refuse to return; Odysseus forces them aboard, ties them down, and the ships sail on.
2 ways setting can impact a text: it creates conflict (the storm, the tempting island); it shapes characters’ actions (the men want to stay); it creates mood (calm after the storm); it can symbolize a theme (the island as an easy escape).
1 thing you wonder: answers vary, e.g. “Would Odysseus have eaten the Lotus if he went ashore himself?”

Look-fors and misconceptions

  • Topic vs. theme. “Temptation” is a topic. Ask: “What does the story say about temptation?”
  • “The Lotus Eaters are villains.” They are not hostile. The danger comes from inside the men, which is why it is a theme about choices.
  • Who is telling this? Odysseus narrates in first person to King Alcinous (in medias res). Extension: could he be making himself look like a better leader than he was?
  • Setting is more than a place. Students should connect the sea and the island to what characters do and to the theme, not just list where it happens.
